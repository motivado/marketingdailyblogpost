# In a World of AI, Your Child's Greatest Advantage Is How They Think

**Date:** 2026-06-06
**Source:** Notion Instagram Idea — Critical Thinking carousel (April 30th, "Draft Image Review")
**Status:** Ready to publish — Selldone API blocked in remote environment (host not in allowlist)

## Plain text

What if the most important question in education right now isn't *what* your child learns—but *how* they learn to think? In a world where AI can recall any fact in seconds, the children who will truly thrive are not the ones who memorize the most—they are the ones who know how to question, analyse, and figure things out.

Critical thinking is the skill that sits behind all other skills. It's the ability to break a problem apart, look at it from different angles, and find a path forward even when there's no clear answer. And the exciting truth is: children don't develop this skill by sitting still and listening. They develop it by *doing*.

Research consistently shows that hands-on, challenge-based learning is one of the most effective ways to build critical thinking in young learners. When a child encounters a problem they've never seen before—like building a machine that moves on its own, or figuring out why their design doesn't work—they are forced to test assumptions, adapt their approach, and try again. Every failed attempt is not a mistake; it's a thinking lesson. Teachers and parents can support this at home too: instead of rushing to give the answer, try asking "What do you think went wrong?" or "What would happen if we changed this part?" These small questions plant seeds of independent thinking that grow over a lifetime.

Practical hands-on STEM activities are especially powerful for building critical thinking in primary and secondary school children. Projects that involve design, testing, and iteration—like constructing a simple machine or programming a robot—naturally develop a child's ability to plan, troubleshoot, and communicate their reasoning. The key is creating low-stakes environments where mistakes are expected, explored, and celebrated as part of the process.

At TokyLabs in Bali, building critical thinkers is at the heart of everything we do. Our **Tokymini** kits are designed specifically for primary school students—screenless, intuitive, and built around the idea that every child deserves to experience the joy of figuring something out on their own. Whether in after-school programmes at international schools or in hands-on STEM classes, our teachers create the kind of safe, curious environment where children stop asking "What's the right answer?" and start asking "What happens if I try this?" Because in today's world, that question is worth everything.
