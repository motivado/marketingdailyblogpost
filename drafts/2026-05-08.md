# What Happens to a Child's Brain at a Hackathon? More Than You'd Expect

Imagine giving your child a real problem, a team, and a deadline — with no textbook in sight. That is exactly what a hackathon does. In a matter of hours, young learners are challenged to design, build, test, and present a solution using whatever tools and creativity they can muster. It sounds intense, and it is. But the growth that happens inside those hours? That is where the real magic begins.

When children work through a hackathon challenge, they are not just learning a subject — they are activating an entire constellation of skills at once. Critical thinking kicks in when the first idea does not work. Communication deepens when teammates need to agree on a direction. Resilience builds every time a prototype fails and the team decides to try again anyway. This is the kind of learning that sticks — not because it was taught, but because it was *lived*.

Research on project-based and challenge-driven learning consistently shows that when students are emotionally invested in solving a real problem, knowledge retention improves dramatically. The best learning experiences share one trait: they feel like play with purpose. At home, you can spark the same creative fire with open-ended science projects. Give your child a simple constraint — build something that can hold the most weight using only straws and tape, or design a paper bridge that carries a coin across a gap — and watch how naturally they begin to think like engineers. The goal is never the perfect solution. It is the process of thinking, experimenting, and discovering what works.

What makes a hackathon especially powerful is the community it creates. Students discover that their teammates' strengths complement their own, and that collaboration produces something none of them could have built alone. This experience of co-creating under pressure is one of the most valuable lessons young people can carry into the future — long after the event itself is over.

At TokyLabs in Bali, the hackathon is one of our favourite ways to bring everything together. This May, young innovators are gathering to take on real-world challenges using robotics and creative technology — not as competitors trying to win, but as a community of makers discovering what they are truly capable of. Whether your child is already passionate about robotics or just curious about what building something real feels like, the TokyLabs Hackathon is the perfect place to find out. Visit tokylabs.com to learn more.
