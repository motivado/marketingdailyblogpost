<p>What would your child do if someone handed them a real problem, a team of peers, and said: <em>"You have until tomorrow to solve this"</em>? The answer might surprise you — and it might be one of the most important questions in education right now. Student hackathons are not just for teen coders or future engineers. They are one of the most powerful learning formats available, and more children than ever are discovering what they are truly capable of when the pressure is on and the creativity is unleashed.</p>

<h2>What a Hackathon Actually Teaches</h2>
<p>A hackathon is a time-limited challenge where participants work in teams to design, build, and present a solution to an open problem. Unlike a school test, there is no single right answer. Unlike a regular class project, the stakes feel real — and that feeling of real consequence is exactly what drives deep learning. When children work through a hackathon, they practise prioritising under pressure, communicating ideas quickly, making decisions with incomplete information, and recovering from setbacks — all within hours rather than weeks. These are the skills that employers, universities, and life itself will demand of them. Crucially, children do not just practise these skills: they <em>feel</em> themselves growing, and that feeling builds lasting confidence.</p>

<h2>How to Help Your Child Get the Most Out of It</h2>
<p>Whether your child is preparing for their first hackathon or still on the fence, a few mindset shifts make all the difference. First, reframe winning. In a hackathon, the team that learns the most often gains more than the team that wins the prize. Encourage your child to focus on what they built and what went wrong, not just the result. Second, celebrate the team. Hackathons are collaborative by design — children learn to depend on others' strengths and to contribute their own, which is a skill that cannot be taught in isolation. Third, follow the curiosity after the event. A hackathon often sparks a new interest — in electronics, in design, in storytelling through technology. That spark is worth nurturing.</p>

<h2>TokyLabs Hackathon: Where Young Inventors Come Alive</h2>
