**What are teachers really struggling with right now?** Not the polished, slide-deck version that shows up in education reports — but the honest, unfiltered kind that comes out when educators are in a room together, trusting each other enough to speak freely.

That is the question TokyLabs founder Eduardo set out to answer when he attended a recent unconference focused on the real challenges facing school teachers and educational organizations today. Unlike traditional conferences, unconferences are participant-driven — the agenda is built in real time by the people in the room, which means conversations tend to go deeper, get more honest, and surface the things that rarely make it into official curriculum documents.

## What Educators Are Genuinely Wrestling With

Across the sessions, a few themes emerged that will feel familiar to anyone working closely with schools. The pace of technological change — particularly AI — is creating real anxiety for teachers who want to prepare students for a future that looks different every year. Many are asking: how do we build skills that will not become obsolete? How do we teach children to think, not just to recall?

There is also a growing recognition that engagement is the real crisis. Students are more distracted than ever, and traditional classroom structures are struggling to hold their attention. The educators who are thriving are those finding ways to make learning physical, collaborative, and genuinely surprising — where the process itself becomes motivating.

## Why Hands-On Learning Keeps Rising to the Top

Time and again, teachers pointed to project-based and hands-on activities as the approaches that actually work. When students build something — a robot, a circuit, a working mechanism — they stop waiting for permission to be curious. They start asking questions because they need the answers, not because they will be tested on them.

This is exactly the learning philosophy that TokyLabs was built on. Our after-school programs and in-school workshops are designed not just to teach robotics, but to put students in situations where curiosity becomes the engine. Tokymaker kits give secondary school students the tools to prototype real ideas, while Tokymini brings hands-on exploration to primary school classrooms without a screen in sight. Our teacher training and STEM certification programs help educators bring this kind of experience into their own practice — confidently, and in a way that fits their school's rhythm.

Showing up at these conversations is not a nice-to-have for TokyLabs. It is how we make sure we are building programs that actually help — not the programs we think schools need, but the ones educators are telling us they do.
