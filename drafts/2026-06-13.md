## Article Body (HTML — ready to publish)

```html
<p>What if the most valuable thing your child could learn right now has nothing to do with memorising facts or even coding? In a world where artificial intelligence can answer any question in seconds, the skill that will truly set children apart is one that machines cannot replicate: <strong>creativity</strong>.</p>

<h2>Creativity Is How Children Think — Not Just What They Make</h2>
<p>When we talk about creativity in education, we do not simply mean drawing or painting. We mean the ability to look at a problem from a fresh angle, to connect ideas that seem unrelated, and to persist through uncertainty until something new emerges. These are exactly the cognitive muscles that a fast-changing, AI-rich world demands.</p>
<p>Research consistently shows that children who are encouraged to <em>try, make mistakes, and figure things out on their own</em> build a growth mindset — the inner confidence to face challenges without fear. When a child builds a robot that does not work on the first attempt, then tinkers until it does, they are not just learning electronics. They are learning that failure is part of the creative process and that their ideas are worth exploring.</p>

<h2>Hands-On Making: Where Creativity Comes Alive</h2>
<p>Creativity does not thrive in passive environments. It flourishes when children are given real materials, open-ended challenges, and the freedom to follow their curiosity. Hands-on robotics and STEM activities are particularly powerful because they are immediate: you build something, you press a button, something happens — and if it is not what you expected, the problem-solving begins.</p>
<p>Here are three simple ways teachers and parents can nurture creative thinking every day:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Ask "What would happen if...?" instead of "What is the right answer?"</strong> — this shifts the focus from recall to exploration.</li>
  <li><strong>Celebrate the process, not only the result.</strong> Display sketches, prototypes, and revised plans alongside finished projects.</li>
  <li><strong>Give children real constraints and real freedom.</strong> A limited set of materials (cardboard, tape, a motor) forces creative solutions far more effectively than an unlimited budget.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Building Tomorrow's Thinkers, One Idea at a Time</h2>
<p>At TokyLabs, creativity sits at the heart of everything we do in Bali and beyond. Our after-school robotics programmes use the <strong>Tokymaker</strong> kit to give secondary school students the tools to bring their own ideas to life — not to follow a script, but to design, build, and iterate. For younger learners in primary school, <strong>Tokymini</strong> removes screens entirely, letting children explore technology through pure physical interaction. Our certified teacher training ensures that educators feel confident guiding this kind of open, joy-filled learning in their own classrooms. When children discover that their ideas have real power — that they can build something that moves, responds, or solves a problem — confidence follows naturally. And confident, creative children are exactly what the future is asking for.</p>
```
