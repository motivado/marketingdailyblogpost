# What Educators Are Really Saying: Lessons from Bali's Education Unconference

<p>What are the real challenges teachers face today? Not the ones polished into a curriculum report or listed on a conference agenda — the raw, honest ones shared between educators who are in the classroom every day, who see what works and what doesn't. At TokyLabs, we believe the only way to build something that truly helps children learn is to listen to the people closest to them: their teachers.</p>

<h2>Going Where the Conversations Are</h2>
<p>That belief recently took Eduardo, TokyLabs' founder, to an education unconference in Bali. Unconferences are different from traditional professional events. There are no polished keynotes or scripted panels. Instead, educators gather to shape the agenda together — raising the questions that actually keep them up at night and working through them collectively. The energy is honest, the conversations are direct, and the insights are real.</p>
<p>What Eduardo heard mattered: teachers are navigating rapid change in their classrooms — the rise of AI tools, the challenge of keeping students genuinely engaged, the pressure to teach 21st-century skills with 20th-century structures. Many feel the gap between what the curriculum asks for and what children actually need to thrive. These are not small problems, and they don't have quick answers. But they are exactly the right problems to be asking about.</p>

<h2>Why Staying Grounded in Real Challenges Matters</h2>
<p>For an edtech company, it is easy to become absorbed in building products and lose touch with the people those products are meant to serve. That's a trap TokyLabs actively avoids. The conversations at the unconference are not just inspirational — they are operational. They inform how we design our robotics kits, how we structure our teacher training programs, and what we prioritize when we develop new learning experiences for children in Bali and beyond.</p>
<p>When a teacher shares that students disengage because they feel like passive recipients of knowledge, that confirms the importance of open-ended, hands-on robotics challenges where children are the ones making decisions. When an educator describes the difficulty of building critical thinking in a culture of right-or-wrong answers, that points directly to why our approach emphasizes exploration over instruction.</p>

<h2>Building in Step with the Real World</h2>
<p>At TokyLabs, keeping our ear to the ground is not a side activity — it is part of how we work. Our after-school programs, Tokymaker kits for secondary students, and Tokymini screenless robotics for younger children are all shaped by what we learn from teachers, parents, and children themselves. If education is changing, we want to be ready for it — not reacting to it months later.</p>
<p>We're grateful to every educator who showed up to that unconference and spoke honestly. Those conversations remind us why this work matters and what it means to truly be on the side of learning.</p>
